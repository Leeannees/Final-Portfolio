Coursework and skills developed at James Madison University.

JMURJ: JMU Undergraduate Research Journal

JMURJ Volume 12

In the JMURJ course, I worked as part of the editorial team on Volumes 12 and 13 of the James Madison Undergraduate Research Journal, reviewing and refining undergraduate research submissions for publication. My role focused on manuscript editing, improving clarity, organization, and adherence to academic style guidelines while preserving each author’s voice and argument. This experience strengthened my ability to evaluate complex writing, provide constructive feedback, and collaborate with peers to produce polished, publication-ready work.

UNST190: Who Cares About Small Farms Anyway?

View presentation (PDF)

LinkedIn post

In the Who Cares About Small Farms Anyway course, I worked on a team project to address the lack of visibility and accessibility of local farms for college students. Through research, surveys, and stakeholder conversations, we identified that while many students value local food systems, they often struggle to access them or do not actively seek out local farms. Our project focused on bridging this gap by creating a model that connects students with local agriculture in a more accessible and engaging way. As part of my work, I contributed to the development of a proposed general education course (AG 101) and a Community Supported Agriculture (CSA) program that would integrate hands-on learning with real-world impact. The model included student volunteer opportunities with local farms, a structured course curriculum on food systems and sustainability, and a revenue sharing CSA system designed to support farmers while remaining accessible to students. This project emphasized community engagement, sustainability, and equitable access to local food, while also demonstrating how universities can partner with local organizations to create long-term impact.

H4D: Hacking for Defense

Vital Vault project page

In the Hacking4Defense course, I worked on a team called Vital Vault, where we were tasked with addressing a complex government “wicked problem” focused on the storage and distribution of humanitarian aid in other countries. Our team researched logistical, political, and environmental challenges, and developed solutions that balanced efficiency, accessibility, and long-term sustainability. This experience strengthened my ability to collaborate on interdisciplinary teams, analyze real-world policy issues, and apply strategic problem-solving to high-stakes global challenges.

ALKU: Government Staffing & Recruiting Internship

ALKU website

During my internship at ALKU, I worked within the company’s government division to support staffing efforts for government prime contractors and build relationships with consultants seeking contract opportunities. My role involved sourcing candidates, conducting outreach, maintaining professional relationships, and helping match consultants with positions based on technical qualifications and project needs. Over the course of 11 months, I developed strong communication, networking, and client relationship skills while gaining experience working in a fast-paced professional environment focused on collaboration, problem-solving, and strategic recruiting.

University Writing Center: Writing Consultant

JMU University Writing Center

As a Writing Consultant at the James Madison University Writing Center, I worked one-on-one with undergraduate and graduate students from a wide range of academic disciplines and backgrounds. During consultations, I assisted students throughout all stages of the writing process, including brainstorming, organization, revision, clarity, and argument development. This experience strengthened my ability to adapt communication styles to different writers, provide constructive feedback, and collaborate with students to improve both their writing and confidence as communicators.
